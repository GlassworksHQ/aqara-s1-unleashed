Pick a direction

Five static mockups of the browser installer that puts S1 Kiosk on a wall panel. Same palette, type and motion as the panel's own setup screen; only the layout and feel change. Each one shows all five steps plus an error state. E merges the parts of A, B and D you liked.

Recommended: merged A + B + D
E

Rail, ring, calm

A's rail keeps where you are on screen, B's big ring makes progress obvious with the detail one tap away, and on a phone it becomes D's one-question flow.

  • One clear progress element per step; the partitions, hashes and log stay under "Show details".
  • One white button per screen, with very large type on "Hold the button" and "Keep it plugged in".
  • Below 900 px the rail turns into D's thin line and "Step 3 of 5", with big buttons pinned at the bottom.

A

Onboarding port

The panel's first-boot screen, moved to the browser: a left rail with the big title and a vertical step list, plus a glass card for the current step.

  • Feels like one product from the browser to the panel.
  • Where you are, and what's done, is always on screen.
  • The rail uses width a small laptop screen might want for content.

B

Centred wizard

A single centred card under a horizontal step bar, with a large picture for each step: the cable, the board photo with a zoomed-in button, a progress ring.

  • The most approachable option: pictures do the explaining.
  • The magnified FEL button makes the trickiest step obvious.
  • Taller pages; the details sit below the picture.

C

Device + console

A drawn panel with live status (USB ID, mode, chip) on the left. Progress bars, a partition table with hashes and a collapsible log on the right.

  • Shows everything it does: IDs, hashes, read-back checks.
  • The log makes bug reports easy, and tinkerers will like it.
  • Dense; can feel technical to a first-timer.

D

Minimal full-bleed

One question per screen, very large type, big buttons and almost no chrome. A thin line at the top shows progress. The arrow keys step through.

  • The calmest and least intimidating; nothing competes with the task.
  • Scales from a phone-sized window to a large screen with no change.
  • Less detail on screen while it works (no per-step log).

How to review. These are static: there's no USB or device code, and the buttons only move between screens. Each mockup has a small bar at the bottom for jumping to any screen, including the error state ("Panel not found in FEL mode"). That bar isn't part of the design. The partition sizes, hashes and timings shown are placeholders.
